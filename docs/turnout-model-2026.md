# Maryland 2026 general election turnout forecast

Mail-in data through October 16, 2026. Generated by `scripts/turnout_model/report.Rmd`; the full report is `docs/turnout-model-2026.html`.

## Statewide ballots cast

- **Low (a 2022-like midterm):** 2,095,147 ballots cast (within-scenario 90% interval 2,053,581 to 2,136,504); 48.7% of active registration, 45.1% of eligible registration (active plus inactive).
- **Central (the level rule's blend):** 2,282,279 ballots cast (within-scenario 90% interval 2,240,832 to 2,323,202); 53.1% of active registration, 49.2% of eligible registration (active plus inactive).
- **High (a 2018-like midterm):** 2,506,337 ballots cast (within-scenario 90% interval 2,465,906 to 2,546,114); 58.3% of active registration, 54.0% of eligible registration (active plus inactive).

The range to quote is 2,053,581 to 2,546,114 ballots cast (low's 5th to high's 95th percentile), 47.7% to 59.2% of active registration. For comparison, 2,031,635 ballots were cast in 2022 (49.3% of active registration, 45.7% of eligible registration) and 2,335,128 in 2018 (59.1%).

## Party mix of the electorate

Central scenario: Democrats 53.7%, Republicans 26.5%, other 19.8% of ballots cast, by registered party. UMBC Institute of Politics likely voters (unweighted): Democrats 60%, Republicans 25%, other 15%.

## Counties (central rate on active registration)

- Highest: Talbot 64.5%; Kent 63.3%; Queen Anne's 60.7%; Howard 60.7%; Carroll 59.2%.
- Lowest: Baltimore City 42.0%; Prince George's 46.9%; Wicomico 50.2%; Charles 51.3%; Washington 51.3%.

## Caveats

- The spread between scenarios is the real uncertainty; within-scenario intervals are narrow by construction.
- The central weights come from a written rule; the mail index has no 2018 analogue and depends on how requests are projected.
- The 2026 primary had an incumbent governor, and its party split is estimated from the voter file.
- Composition is centred within party because the voter file lacks the 2022 voters who died or moved.
- Not modelled: election-day weather, late changes in the races, changes to mail-voting rules, registration after September.
- The 2014 general is left out.
- The board's 2018-2024 sheets divide by active registration; its 2026 guide divides by all eligible voters. Both are shown.
