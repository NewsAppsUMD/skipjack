# Maryland 2026 general election turnout forecast

Mail-in data through October 30, 2026; early voting and same-day registration through October 29, 2026. Generated by `scripts/turnout_model/report.Rmd`; the full report is `docs/turnout-model-2026.html`.

## Statewide ballots cast

- **Low (a 2022-like midterm):** 2,102,288 ballots cast (within-scenario 90% interval 2,060,722 to 2,143,646); 48.9% of active registration, 45.3% of eligible registration (active plus inactive, projected).
- **Central (the level rule's blend):** 2,330,865 ballots cast (within-scenario 90% interval 2,289,592 to 2,371,828); 54.2% of active registration, 50.2% of eligible registration (active plus inactive, projected).
- **High (a 2018-like midterm):** 2,513,478 ballots cast (within-scenario 90% interval 2,473,047 to 2,553,256); 58.4% of active registration, 54.1% of eligible registration (active plus inactive, projected).

The range to quote is 2,060,722 to 2,553,256 ballots cast (low's 5th to high's 95th percentile), 47.9% to 59.4% of active registration. For comparison, 2,031,635 ballots were cast in 2022 (49.3% of active registration, 45.7% of eligible registration) and 2,335,128 in 2018 (59.1%).

## Ballots already cast

604,396 early votes (unofficial, voting over) and 376,967 mail ballots received by October 30, 2026, projected to 560,543 mail ballots in all: 1,164,939 ballots banked before election day, 26% more than 2022's 922,864. The central forecast needs 37.2% of the registrants who have not yet voted to vote on November 3 (2022: 34.6%; 2018: 48.9%). Same-day registration beyond 2022's level adds 7,142 ballots to every scenario.

## Party mix of the electorate

Central scenario: Democrats 53.9%, Republicans 26.2%, other 19.9% of ballots cast, by registered party. UMBC Institute of Politics likely voters (unweighted): Democrats 60%, Republicans 25%, other 15%.

## Counties (central rate on active registration)

- Highest: Talbot 65.4%; Kent 64.3%; Howard 61.8%; Queen Anne's 61.7%; Carroll 60.1%.
- Lowest: Baltimore City 43.3%; Prince George's 48.2%; Wicomico 51.3%; Washington 52.3%; Charles 52.4%.

## Caveats

- The spread between scenarios is the real uncertainty; within-scenario intervals are narrow by construction.
- The central weights come from a written rule; the mail and banked-ballot indices have no 2018 analogue, and the banked index counts voters who moved from election day to early voting as engagement.
- The early-voting count is the board's unofficial tally; late mail is projected at 2022's rate of arrival.
- The 2026 primary had an incumbent governor, and its party split is estimated from the voter file.
- Composition is centred within party because the voter file lacks the 2022 voters who died or moved.
- Not modelled: election-day weather, late changes in the races, changes to mail-voting rules, registration after September other than same-day registration.
- The 2014 general is left out.
- The board's 2018-2024 sheets divide by active registration; its 2026 guide divides by all eligible voters. Both are shown.
